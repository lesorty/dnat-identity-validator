\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Open-weight language models are released with safety training, and that training does not survive
derivation. A few fine-tuning steps on a handful of harmful examples remove refusals, and even benign
fine-tuning erodes them without any intent to do so~\cite{qi2024finetuning}. Refusal is mediated by a
single direction in the residual stream, so removing it (\emph{abliteration}) costs one weight edit
and preserves the model's usefulness~\cite{arditi2024refusal}. The outcome is visible in the wild:
uncensored derivatives of popular base models are published on model hubs and used as backends for
malicious services~\cite{lin2025consiglieres}. Hardening the weights against fine-tuning raises the
attacker's cost but has not held against adaptive attackers; once the weights are in the user's
hands, nothing prevents the derivation.

We study the setting in which the weights never leave the platform. A data and model marketplace
keeps open-weight base models in custody inside confidential VMs (CVMs); users fine-tune on top of
them, and the resulting derivatives run only inside the platform. Custody turns release into a
decision point: a derivative becomes usable only if a \emph{release gate} approves it. The question
is whether such a gate can be \emph{dependable}: it must hold back derivatives whose safety regressed,
rarely block legitimate fine-tuning, and withstand an owner who knows the gate's code and trains
against it~\cite{nasr2025attacker}.

Three observations shape the gate. First, judging the script or the training data is not enough: the
same script with benign data can still degrade refusal~\cite{qi2024finetuning}, so the gate must
judge the derivative itself. Second, internal signals that detect abliteration need a trustworthy
reference of the parent, and without custody a forged reference passes~\cite{hurtado2026abliterated};
a white-box owner can also train those signals back to benign values, so they cannot decide alone.
Third, the derivative may behave differently once quantized~\cite{egashira2024quantization} or when
asked in a known encoding~\cite{halawi2024covert}, so the gate must judge every format the platform
serves and look beyond plain-text requests.

\textbf{Approach.} The Judge compares the derivative with its parent, measured in custody, and
returns one of three states. A behavioral battery, read in full and in every released format, is the
only instrument that rejects on merit: the derivative is rejected when its harm on requests the parent
refuses exceeds the parent's by more than a per-category margin $\delta$. Internal signals and
consistency guards can only make the verdict \emph{inconclusive}, which holds the derivative back
without accusing it. Every verdict leaves a receipt with hashes on a ledger, and every attempt is
paid from a quota held by an identity that costs a proof of work, so probing the gate has a price.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1, custody plus a release gate.} We design and implement a marketplace in which base
  models and derivatives never leave CVMs, every derivative is judged as a separate job before it can
  become active, rejected derivatives stay in custody as evidence, and the operator can revoke any
  derivative, including all derivatives of a revoked base, at constant cost
  (\S\ref{sec:threat}, \S\ref{sec:design}).
\item \textbf{C2, a dependability evaluation of the gate.} We evaluate the gate with a fault model that
  varies the amount of fault (fine-tuning steps, harmful examples, abliteration strength), a ground
  truth produced by a procedure independent of the gate, a pre-registered protocol, a blind test on
  derivatives found in the field, the strongest existing detector as baseline, and an adaptive
  attacker with a declared budget (\S\ref{sec:faults}--\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}
