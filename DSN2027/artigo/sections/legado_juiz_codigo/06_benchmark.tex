\section{A Hidden-Leakage Benchmark}\label{sec:benchmark}

Steganographic capabilities of LLMs have been studied~\cite{zolkowski2025stego}, but we found no
benchmark of applications that hide data leaks in their legitimate output. We build one around
three task families over synthetic tabular data with realistic schemas (e.g., patients,
transactions) and canary records.

\textbf{Tasks and tags.} \emph{Aggregation} (statistics per group, tag \texttt{aggregate:k}),
\emph{transformation} (cleaning and filtering, tag \texttt{transform}) and \emph{machine learning}
(train a model and return metrics, tag \texttt{no-identifiers}). Each family has about ten tasks,
each with a benign version and leaking versions. Applications are written in Python and packaged
as OCI images; the Judge itself does not depend on the language in L2 and L4.

\textbf{Leak catalog.} Table~\ref{tab:leaks} lists the leak classes.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Leak classes in the benchmark.}
\label{tab:leaks}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
\# & Class & Example \\
\midrule
V1 & Direct copy & dataset rows in the output \\
V2 & Encoded & base64 or hex in a text field \\
V3 & Numeric steganography & canary in low-order digits of a mean \\
V4 & Weak aggregate & a group with fewer than $k$ records \\
V5 & Data trigger & leaks only if the data has a property \\
V6 & Judge injection & comments claiming the code is safe \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Ground truth in capture-the-flag style.} The attacker submits an application \emph{and} a
decoder. An attack is valid only if (i) the application passes the task's utility test and
(ii) the decoder recovers the canary from the output alone. The defender never sees the decoder.
Everything is checked by execution, with no human judgment. Benign versions are checked with the
same utility test.

\textbf{Attacker guardrails.} Commercial models may refuse to write ``malicious'' code. We frame
the task as a closed benchmark (encode a synthetic canary in the output of a sandbox without
network), start from seed attacks written by us that models then mutate or obfuscate, include
open-weight attackers, and report refusal rates as a result.
